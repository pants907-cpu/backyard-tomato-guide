Plant Care

Steady care grows stronger plants.

Tomatoes respond best to consistency. Build a routine, then let the plant tell you what it needs.

The weekly routine

Four habits that matter.

Check plants often. A two-minute look every day makes small problems easier to correct.

Water

Deep and steady

Water at the base when the top inch or two of soil begins to dry.

Feed

Follow the plant

Use compost or tomato fertilizer according to the label. Avoid too much nitrogen.

Support

Lift the vines

Tie stems loosely or guide them through a cage to keep fruit off the soil.

Prune

Keep airflow

Remove damaged lower leaves without stripping away leaves that shade fruit.

Troubleshooting

Start with what you can see.

Yellow leaves

Check moisture first. Both soggy roots and very dry soil can cause yellowing.

Blossom-end rot

Uneven watering can keep the plant from taking up calcium steadily.

Split fruit

Heavy watering after a dry spell can swell fruit too quickly.

Chewed leaves

Look under leaves and along stems for hornworms and other insects.

Wilting

Check soil a few inches down and notice whether the plant recovers after sunset.
