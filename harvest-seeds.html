Harvest and Seeds

Enjoy this harvest. Save the next one.

Pick at the right moment, handle fruit gently, and turn one favorite tomato into next season's possibilities.

Ready to pick

Look beyond color.

A ripe tomato has developed the expected color for its variety, feels slightly soft under gentle pressure, and releases without a hard pull. Striped, purple, yellow, and green-when-ripe tomatoes may need more than color alone.

Three ripeness clues

  1. Color: Developed for the variety
  2. Feel: Firm with a slight give
  3. Release: Easy to twist or clip
Pick

Protect the plant

Hold the fruit instead of pulling the vine. Twist gently or use clean clippers.

Store

Keep the flavor

Store uncut tomatoes at room temperature and out of direct sunlight.

Check

Catch damage early

Remove split or damaged fruit promptly and use safe portions soon.

Seed saving

Seven steps from tomato to seed packet.

  1. 01

    Select

    Choose a fully ripe, healthy tomato from a plant with traits you want to keep.

  2. 02

    Remove

    Squeeze the seeds and gel into a small labeled container.

  3. 03

    Ferment

    Add a little water and leave the loosely covered container for two to four days.

  4. 04

    Rinse

    Add water, let good seeds settle, pour off floating material, and repeat.

  5. 05

    Dry

    Spread seeds in one layer and let them dry completely.

  6. 06

    Label

    Record the variety or description and the year.

  7. 07

    Store

    Keep dry seeds in a paper envelope in a cool, dark, dry container.

Important: Seeds must be completely dry before storage. Trapped moisture can cause mold and ruin the batch.

Before storing

The final seed check.

  • Seeds feel hard and do not bend
  • No gel remains
  • Envelope has a name and year
  • Storage spot is cool, dark, and dry
  • Seeds from different plants stay separated
